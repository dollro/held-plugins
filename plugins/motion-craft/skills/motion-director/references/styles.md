# Styles

Seven premium styles for launch films. Each is a way of drawing and moving the pictures, not a preset. At stage 2 suggest 3-4 that suit the film; at stage 4 each picked style becomes a direction, drawn as stills of the same beat. A film has one drawing style; footage and real UI sit inside it.

**Made with**: *frames* = built in the composition from type, shapes and lines; *stills* = generated or supplied still images moved in the build (no clip tool needed); *clips* = generated footage (shot-forge) or supplied footage; *ui* = real screen recordings.

| Style | What it looks like | Best for roles | Made with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product close-up | the real product UI, cropped close (1.6-2×) on a flat brand ground; the camera cycles between the whole screen, one pane and one control | proof, star, pillar (as context) | ui + frames |
| Kinetic statement | statements and numbers are the picture; large type (6-30% of frame height) on flat colour fields that change per act | cost, turn, versus, close | frames |
| One-accent minimal | ink, ground and grey; the hero colour only on what matters; generous empty space | reveal, cost, close; serious or premium brands | frames |
| Graded footage and type | footage with one grade for the whole film (time of day, warmth, grain); statements land over it in a fixed home position | moment, villain, outcome, voice | clips + frames |
| Modular grid | lines and type slide on a strict grid; panels open and close along it | pillar, objection, versus; precise, technical products | frames |
| Object morph | one shape keeps becoming the next (a photo into a page, a page into a phone, an arrow into the mark) | reveal, pillar, transitions into the star | frames |
| Layered stills | photos or generated stills split into 2-4 depth layers that move at different speeds | moment, villain, outcome when there is no clip tool | stills |

## Per style

### Product close-up
- **Numbers**: UI text ≥ 26 px at 1080p after the crop; camera moves 0.4-0.6 s; holds of 3-10 s while the UI acts.
- **Moves**: Punch-in, Settle, UI acts; Morph and Zoom through as seams.
- **Needs**: a real recording or a test account for every proof and star beat.
- **Risk**: panning across a still capture does not show the product doing anything; a small window in an empty frame is not a shot.

### Kinetic statement
- **Numbers**: statement 6-9% of frame height, one hero word; a single number up to 30%; one cadence for word builds across the film.
- **Moves**: Land, Build, Count, Word swap, Colour flood.
- **Needs**: heading font files.
- **Risk**: a film of only type loses the feeling; pair it with footage or UI in the villain and star frames.

### One-accent minimal
- **Numbers**: ≥ 40% of every frame empty; hero colour on one element per frame; grey for everything secondary.
- **Moves**: Land, Hold, Blur out; slow and few.
- **Needs**: a brand-lock with a strong hero colour.
- **Risk**: empty becomes dark void; keep the ground a brand colour, not black by default.

### Graded footage and type
- **Numbers**: one grade (e.g. dusk, warm practicals, 3% grain); shots 3-7 s with a Push of ≤ 4%; type in one home position.
- **Moves**: Push, Match cut, Carry into and out of footage.
- **Needs**: shot-forge with a clip tool, or supplied footage; a continuity sheet for any person who recurs.
- **Risk**: generated faces in close-up; screens or text inside generated shots (composite them instead).

### Modular grid
- **Numbers**: 12 columns, margins at the brand-lock safe margin; lines 2 px; panels open on `power2.inOut`.
- **Moves**: lines draw, panels slide along the grid, Word swap inside cells.
- **Needs**: nothing beyond brand-lock.
- **Risk**: three equal cells in a row reads as feature cards; one cell leads per frame.

### Object morph
- **Numbers**: one morph per seam, 0.2-0.7 s; the morphing object keeps the hero colour or the ink, never both.
- **Moves**: Morph, Collapse into, Grow through, Mark lands.
- **Needs**: a recurring object that belongs to the story (a photo tile, a report page, an arrow).
- **Risk**: abstract shapes that stand for nothing; the object must be something the story names.

### Layered stills
- **Numbers**: 2-4 layers; nearest layer moves 2-3× the farthest; ≤ 6% total travel per shot.
- **Moves**: Push with parallax, Carry between stills.
- **Needs**: generated or supplied stills with clean separation (subject, mid, ground).
- **Risk**: cardboard cut-out look; keep travel small and the edges soft.

## Choosing

- A film with a `star` or `proof` beat: Product close-up is one of the suggestions.
- No clip tool: offer Layered stills instead of Graded footage and type.
- Label directions by their style, not by a film, filmmaker or brand.
- Combine at most two: one for the world (footage, stills or type) and Product close-up for the product.
