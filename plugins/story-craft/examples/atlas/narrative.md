# Site reports, finally done on site.

Illustrative example · Fictional company (Acme) and sample data.

## Why this story

Purpose: site reports are an evening chore that comes after the real work → site reports can be finished on site, before you leave.
Audience: site managers on mid-size builds; they call it "the paperwork after the work". The head of operations decides.
The one belief it must change: "The report is something you do later, at the desk."

## Storylines considered

1. **The evening pile** (picked): a moment in someone's day. Opens on a phone gallery scrolling past dozens of site photos in a dark car, then the empty report form. Beats: pile → cost → what if → atlas → capture, understand, one tap → live demo → home on time → pilot. Why: every site manager has lived this exact evening, so a stranger follows it from the first second.
2. **Report first**: the result first. Opens on a finished, signed report, then pulls back to show it was made in the car park four minutes earlier. Strong for the film, weaker on stage where the reveal comes too early.
3. **One defect's journey** (the bold one): one object across apps. A single crack in a wall travels from a photo to a voice memo to a plan to the client's inbox, without anyone typing. Memorable, but needs more generated footage than the budget allows.
