# Arc patterns

Beat sequences that work, in any medium. A beat is one idea; on stage it is usually one slide, in a film one scene of 4-7 s.

## Sequences

**Product launch (14-18 beats, 10-20 min on stage)**
1. moment: the situation everyone knows (is)
2. villain: concrete, countable (is)
3. cost: the villain in one number (is)
4. moment or villain: the second blow, the consequence (is)
5. turn: "What if …?" (could)
6. reveal: the name alone (could)
7-9. pillar ×3: what it does, one per beat (could)
10-11. proof: one number or test per pillar that needs one (could)
12. star: the demo or the before/after (could)
13. objection: price, privacy, fit answered in one beat (could)
14. versus: before vs after in one unit (is → could)
15. voice: a real user (could)
16. outcome: the new week (could)
17. close: availability, price, the ask (could)

**Vision or strategy talk (10-14 beats)**
World today (is) → the shift underway (cost or proof number) → what it means for us (could) → three bets (pillar ×3) → proof each bet is real → what we ask of you → the picture of success (outcome, close).

**Feature or update launch (6-10 beats)**
What users told us (voice or cost) → the one change (reveal) → how it works (pillar ×3 or one star demo) → the result (versus) → available when, for whom (close).

**Pitch (8-12 beats, 5-10 min)**
Villain → cost → reveal → how it works (pillar ×3) → traction (proof) → why we win (versus, without naming competitors on screen) → team (one beat) → the ask (close).

## Wording patterns

- Headline: "<Company> <verb> <familiar thing>" · "<Thing>, finally." · "The <category> that <benefit>."
- Villain: count the pain: "47 photos. 3 voice memos. Zero report."
- Turn: "What if <the pain> <disappeared / did itself>?"
- Number: the number alone, then "<what it measures> · <who / when>", then a comparison: "That's <familiar unit>."
- Versus: "<old> → <new>" with two numbers in the same unit.
- Pillars: verbs or one-word benefits, parallel in form ("Capture anything. Understands the site. Report in one tap.").
- Close: "Available <when>. <Price>. <Where to start>."
- Avoid: "unlock", "seamless", "supercharge", "game-changer", "revolutionize", "effortless", "in seconds" unless measured. The first beat is never "Introducing" or a logo.

## Making numbers meaningful

| Raw | Meaningful |
|---|---|
| 5 GB storage | 1,000 songs in your pocket |
| 6.5 hours per week | most of a working day, every week |
| 0.3 s latency | faster than you can blink |
| 91% same day | nine out of ten reports leave before the site manager does |

Choose comparisons from the audience's world; check the arithmetic. Never derive a claim the source did not make ("40 min to 2 min" is not "20× faster" unless someone measured both).

## Credibility

Bold claims without proof cost more than they win. So: every claim beat has evidence or softened wording; dates and prices are stated or deliberately left out, never fudged; quotes are real and attributable; comparisons name the benchmark and the test conditions.

## Tells of a generic story

- a list of features with no villain;
- the reveal in the first beat ("Introducing …");
- three pillars that are not parallel, or five;
- a close that asks for nothing;
- a STAR moment that is spectacle unconnected to the headline;
- numbers without a comparison, or comparisons without arithmetic.
