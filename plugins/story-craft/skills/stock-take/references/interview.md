# Interview guide

One question per message. Reflect the answer in one line, then ask the next. Use the owner's language; the examples below are English but the interview runs in whatever language the owner writes.

## 1. Purpose

Ask: "Picture the audience before it starts. What do they believe about <topic> today? And what should they believe when it ends?"

Good answer: two beliefs, the second one in conflict with the first. "Site reports are a necessary evening chore" → "Site reports can be finished on site, before you leave."
Follow-up if vague ("they should know our new product"): "What would they say to a colleague the next morning?"

## 2. Ask

Ask: "What is the one thing you want them to do afterwards?"

Good answer: a single action with a place or date. "Start a free pilot this quarter."
Follow-up if several: "If they do only one, which one?"

## 3. Audience

Ask: "Who is in the room or watching? Describe one of them: role, what their week looks like, how they would describe the pain in their own words."

Good answer: a role in a situation, plus a phrase they actually use. "Site managers on mid-size builds; Friday evenings go to reports; they call it 'the paperwork after the work'."
Follow-up: "Is the person who decides the same person who uses it?"

## 4. Outputs

Ask: "Which pieces do we need from this story: a live talk, a film, a short teaser?"

Offer the typical set if the owner hesitates: keynote (live, 10-20 min), film-60 (landing page, no speaker), teaser-20 (social, muted autoplay). Then propose channel, runtime and speaker for each in one line and let the owner correct it, rather than asking three more questions. Give each an ID; lower-case, hyphenated, runtime in the name for films.

## 5. Evidence

Ask: "Which claims do you want to make? Let's go through them one by one: what is behind each?"

For each claim, get: the evidence (number, quote, result), the source (survey with n, analytics, interview, benchmark with conditions), and then tag strength yourself and say it out loud: "That one I'd mark as likely: three interviews, no measurement."
Follow-ups:
- "Faster / cheaper / better than what, measured how?"
- "Can we name the customer, or only the industry?"
- "Is that number from before or after the last product change?"

Do not argue the owner out of a claim. Record it with the honest strength; the narrative decides how to word it.

## 6. Assets

Ask: "What can we show? Brand files, the live product, recordings, images."

Checklist to walk through briefly:
- design guide or master (.pptx), logo files (SVG or PNG), font files;
- live product URL with a test account; the click path for the demo moment, as steps;
- existing screen recordings and product images;
- voice: who speaks or which VO language and voice; music constraints (licensed library, none).

## Brand lock (only without design guide or master)

Four questions, one at a time:
1. "Which colours, and what is each for? (main, accent, background, text)"
2. "Which fonts for headings and body, and do we have the font files?"
3. "Where are the logo files, and is there a version for dark backgrounds?"
4. "What must never happen with the brand? (stretched logo, other colours, certain words)"

## 7. Constraints

Ask: "Is there anything we must not say or show? Legal lines, claims, competitors' names."
Follow-up, as its own message: "What is the deadline, and is there a budget for generated footage?"

## Closing the interview

Read back the five-line summary. Ask: "Anything wrong or missing?" Fix, then write `intake.md`. (Same order as SKILL.md § Output.)
